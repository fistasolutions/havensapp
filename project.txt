	•  Core functionality: An interactive AI coach using evidence-based techniques like CBT (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy) and ACT (Acceptance and Commitment Therapy) for guided conversations. Users can vent, get coping strategies, or receive quick mood boosts via text-based chats.
	•  Why it works: This is the “viable” heart of your app, providing immediate, private support without human therapists, which users love for accessibility.   Limit to 5-10 predefined conversation flows in beta (e.g., anxiety relief, stress management) to keep it simple.
	•  Beta tweaks: Add basic personalization based on user inputs, with long-term memory for follow-up chats.

	•  Features: Quick check-ins with emotion labels (e.g., 10-20 options like happy, anxious, overwhelmed), visual mood wheels, and trend graphs over time. Integrate with the chatbot for contextual prompts (e.g., “Based on your low mood, try this exercise”).
	•  Why it works: Mood tracking is a staple in mental health apps, helping users build self-awareness and patterns, which drives retention.    It’s low-effort for users and provides data for app improvements.
	•  Beta tweaks: Offline access and simple export options for sharing with therapists.
3.  Guided Journaling Prompts
	•  Features: AI-generated prompts for reflection (e.g., “What triggered your stress today?”), tied to mood logs. Include sentiment analysis on entries to suggest insights or reframing.
	•  Why it works: Combines self-reflection with AI assistance, making it engaging and therapeutic without complexity.   Users from similar apps appreciate this for habit-building.
	•  Beta tweaks: 10-15 templates focused on common themes like gratitude or goal-setting; keep it text-only for MVP.

	•  Features: Short, on-demand exercises like breathing guides, mindfulness tips, or positive affirmations, accessible via the chatbot or a simple menu.

Scripts for Additional User Roles in Hevan MVP
To expand the Hevan MVP for beta testing, we can incorporate tailored chatbot scripts (conversation flows and prompts) for different user roles or scenarios: Providers (e.g., therapists or healthcare professionals using the app for client support), Partners (romantic partners seeking relationship-focused wellness), Family and Friends (group support for interpersonal dynamics), and Kids (child-friendly, age-appropriate interactions, assuming 8-12 years old with parental supervision). These scripts build on the core MVP features (AI chatbot, mood tracking, journaling, self-help) by adding role-specific dialogue trees. They emphasize empathy, evidence-based techniques, and safety disclaimers.
For your Upwork expert, these can be implemented as modular branches in the chatbot logic—e.g., users select a “mode” at onboarding or via prompts, triggering relevant responses. Keep scripts concise for beta (3-5 turns per flow), using natural language processing to handle variations. Include branching based on user inputs for personalization.
Scripts for Providers (Professional Mode)
This mode positions the chatbot as a supplementary tool for therapists/doctors, offering quick client check-in scripts, resource suggestions, or post-session reflections. Focus on HIPAA-compliant privacy and non-diagnostic advice.
Example Flow 1: Client Mood Check-In
•  User (Provider): “Check in on my client’s anxiety today.”
•  Chatbot: “Sure, as a support tool, I can guide a quick mood assessment. Ask your client: ‘On a scale of 1-10, how anxious are you feeling right now?’ Based on their response, suggest a grounding exercise like 4-7-8 breathing. Remember, I’m not a substitute for your professional judgment.”
•  User Follow-Up (e.g., “They said 7.”): “That’s moderate-high. Recommend noting triggers in their journal. Here’s a CBT prompt: ‘What evidence supports or challenges this anxious thought?’ Share this via the app’s export feature if needed.”
•  Branching: If low mood, suggest escalating to in-person care.
Example Flow 2: Resource Recommendation
•  User: “Suggest coping strategies for depression.”
•  Chatbot: “For your client, try these evidence-based options: Daily mood tracking to spot patterns, or ACT exercises like ‘defusion’ from thoughts. I can generate a personalized prompt: ‘Label your thought as “just a thought” and observe it.’ Always integrate with your therapy plan.”
Implementation Notes: Add a “Provider Login” with verification (e.g., code). Limit to read-only client data sharing.
Scripts for Partners (Relationship Wellness Mode)
Tailored for couples or partners dealing with shared emotional challenges, emphasizing communication tools and joint mood tracking to strengthen bonds.
Example Flow 1: Conflict Resolution
•  User (Partner): “We’re arguing about stress again.”
•  Chatbot: “I’m here to help de-escalate. First, rate your current emotion: Angry, Frustrated, Hurt? Let’s reframe: ‘What need isn’t being met right now?’ Suggest sharing this with your partner calmly, like ‘I feel overwhelmed when…’”
•  User Follow-Up (e.g., “Frustrated.”): “Got it. Try a couples exercise: Each share one positive from the day. If needed, journal separately then discuss. Building empathy can reduce conflicts by 30% based on relationship studies.”
•  Branching: If high tension, recommend a timeout with breathing guides.
Example Flow 2: Shared Mood Boost
•  User: “How to support my partner’s anxiety?”
•  Chatbot: “Great initiative! Start with active listening: ‘Tell me more about what’s worrying you.’ Then, co-track moods in the app for patterns. Here’s a joint prompt: ‘What can we do together to feel more connected today?’”
Implementation Notes: Enable “Partner Pairing” for shared (consensual) journals. Use CBT for reframing relationship thoughts.
Scripts for Family and Friends (Social Support Mode)
Designed for group dynamics, like family therapy lite or friend check-ins, focusing on collective wellness and boundary-setting.
Example Flow 1: Family Check-In
•  User (Family Member): “Family tension is high.”
•  Chatbot: “Families can be complex—let’s start small. Everyone rate their mood anonymously via the app. Common prompt: ‘What’s one thing stressing you, and one thing you’re grateful for?’ This builds understanding without blame.”
•  User Follow-Up (e.g., “Kids are withdrawn.”): “Encourage open sharing with ‘I notice you’ve seemed quiet—want to talk?’ Suggest a group activity like a gratitude circle. If ongoing, consider professional family counseling.”
•  Branching: For friends, adapt to “How to check on a friend?” with empathy scripts.
Example Flow 2: Boundary Setting
•  User: “How to set boundaries with friends?”
•  Chatbot: “Boundaries protect your wellness. Practice saying: ‘I appreciate your concern, but I need space right now.’ Journal your feelings post-interaction to reinforce. Friends who respect this strengthen bonds.”
Implementation Notes: Add multi-user invites for group mood polls (anonymous). Incorporate ACT for accepting family differences.
Scripts for Kids (Child-Friendly Mode)
Simplified, fun interactions for children, with parental oversight. Use playful language, short responses, and visual elements (e.g., emojis). Avoid complex topics; focus on basic emotions and positivity.
Example Flow 1: Mood Check-In
•  User (Kid): “I feel sad today.”
•  Chatbot: “Oh no, sad feelings can be tough!   Like a cloud over your head? Pick an emoji that matches:   or  . Now, let’s chase it away—what’s one fun thing you did today?”
•  User Follow-Up (e.g., “Played with my dog.”): “Yay!   That sounds awesome. Draw or tell me about it in your journal. Remember, feelings come and go like weather—tomorrow might be sunny!”
•  Branching: If angry, suggest “cool-down” like counting to 10.
Example Flow 2: Friendship Advice
•  User: “My friend was mean.”
•  Chatbot: “Friends sometimes make mistakes.   How did it make you feel? Try saying: ‘That hurt my feelings—can we play nicely?’ If it’s bully stuff, tell a grown-up. You’re brave for sharing!”
Implementation Notes: Require parent setup; use age-gated simple UI with pictures. Limit to positive reinforcement; include parent alerts for concerning inputs.
Integration into MVP
•  Onboarding: Add a role selector (e.g., “Who are you using this for? Provider / Partner / Family-Friends / Kid”) to trigger scripts.

Key Non-CBT Evidence-Based Practices to Add to Hevan Chatbot
Based on the practices integrated into similar apps, here are actionable takeaways you can pull for Hevan’s MVP or beta expansions. These complement CBT by offering diverse tools for emotional regulation, resilience, and self-care. Focus on implementing them via chatbot scripts, guided exercises, or prompts to keep the app engaging and versatile. I’ve grouped them by practice, noting which apps they draw from and why they’re effective (with evidence highlights).
Mindfulness and Meditation
•  Takeaways: Add short, guided sessions for present-moment awareness, such as 1-5 minute breathing exercises (e.g., 4-7-8 technique) or body scans. Integrate into daily check-ins where the chatbot prompts: “Let’s pause—focus on your breath for 30 seconds.” Track user progress with simple streaks.
•  From Apps: Wysa (reduces stress via mindfulness packs), Liven (calming techniques for burnout), Ahead (relaxation exercises), Clarity (mindfulness for resilience), Woebot (daily focus tools), Youper (mood boosters), Reflectly (venting with awareness).
•  Why Add It: Backed by RCTs showing 20-30% reductions in anxiety; enhances emotional regulation without cognitive restructuring.
Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) Elements
•  Takeaways: Include distress tolerance skills like “TIPP” (Temperature, Intense exercise, Paced breathing, Progressive relaxation) or emotion regulation modules. Chatbot could guide: “When feelings peak, try this: Splash cold water on your face—what changed?” Add journaling for identifying opposites (e.g., acceptance vs. change).
•  From Apps: Wysa (distress tolerance), Clarity (emotion skills), Woebot (regulation and interpersonal tools).
•  Why Add It: Proven for borderline personality and mood disorders; meta-analyses show efficacy in reducing self-harm behaviors by 40%.
Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT)
•  Takeaways: Incorporate “defusion” exercises (e.g., labeling thoughts as “just thoughts”) and value-based goal-setting. Prompt users: “Notice that anxious thought without judging—now, what action aligns with your values today?” Tie to mood tracking for long-term commitment.
•  From Apps: Wysa (acceptance tools), Ahead (value-driven actions), Clarity (acceptance prompts), Youper (emotion acceptance for relationships).
•  Why Add It: Supported by over 300 studies; improves psychological flexibility, reducing depression symptoms by 25-35%.
Positive Psychology and Gratitude Practices
•  Takeaways: Build in daily gratitude prompts (e.g., “Name three things you’re thankful for”) or strength-spotting exercises. Use AI to generate personalized affirmations based on user logs, like “Based on your wins, you’re resilient—build on that.”
•  From Apps: Wysa (optimism building), Liven (self-reflection for motivation), Ahead (resilience activities), How We Feel (self-care routines), Reflectly (motivational quotes and prompts).
•  Why Add It: Harvard studies link gratitude to 10-20% happiness boosts; effective for habit-building and preventing burnout.
Social and Emotional Learning (SEL) / Emotional Literacy
•  Takeaways: Expand mood labeling with RULER framework (Recognize, Understand, Label, Express, Regulate). Chatbot could ask: “Label your emotion precisely—stressed or overwhelmed? How can you express it healthily?” Add kid-friendly versions with emojis.
•  From Apps: How We Feel (Mood Meter-based tracking), Woebot (social skills).
•  Why Add It: Yale’s RULER program shows 11% academic and emotional improvements; great for all ages, including family/partner modes.
Breathwork and Coping Skills
•  Takeaways: Quick, standalone tools like progressive muscle relaxation or box breathing. Integrate as on-demand: “Feeling tense? Inhale for 4, hold for 4, exhale for 4—repeat three times.”
•  From Apps: Liven (breathing for centering), Clarity (coping for stress), Youper (immediate relief guides).
•  Why Add It: NHS-endorsed for panic reduction; physiological benefits backed by biofeedback research.
Implementation Tips for Hevan
•  Integration: Layer these into existing MVP features—e.g., chatbot branches for “Quick Relief” (breathwork/DBT) or “Daily Boost” (positive psychology/ACT). Start with 2-3 in beta to test engagement.
•  Customization: Use user roles (providers, partners, etc.) to adapt—e.g., SEL for kids, ACT for relationships.
•  Evidence Focus: Always include disclaimers and link to resources; aim for 24/7 accessibility like Wysa/Woebot.
•  Beta Testing: Measure via feedback on symptom reduction (e.g., pre/post mood scores) to validate additions.
